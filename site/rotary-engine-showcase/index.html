TRIAD R3Triple-rotor powertrain · Concept

Three rotors.
Zero pistons.

A 1,962 cc rotary engine with no valves, cams or connecting rods. It has four moving parts in its core and fires every 120° of shaft rotation. Scroll to take it apart.

01Ancillaries

First, everything bolted to the outside.

The intake plenum and its six runners lift away from the side ports. The 3-into-1 header comes off the exhaust ports, the six spark plugs back out of the hot side, and the sump and front pulley drop clear.

02Unstack

No block. No head. Just a stack.

The R3 is built as a sandwich of eight slabs: a front cover, a front housing, three rotor housings, two intermediate housings and a rear housing, all clamped by ten tension bolts. Pull the bolts and the slabs slide apart along the shaft axis.

  • Housings8
  • Tension bolts10
  • Eccentric shaft1
03The cycle

Four strokes. One continuous motion.

Each flank of the rotor forms its own sealed chamber with the housing. Scrolling turns the shaft through 1,080°, which is one full turn of the rotor. Follow Chamber A through the cycle.

    04Teardown

    Only four parts actually move.

    The whole working core is three rotors and one eccentric shaft. Lift the rotors off their lobes, release the seals that ride the trochoid, and slide the stationary gears off their housings. Nothing else in the engine moves.

    07Installed

    Mounted low, behind the axle.

    At 620 mm long, the R3 fits entirely behind the front axle line. That puts its mass inside the wheelbase, close to the middle of the car, and the shallow sump lets the shaft sit just 410 mm off the ground.
